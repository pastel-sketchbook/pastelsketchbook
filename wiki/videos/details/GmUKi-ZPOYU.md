## Summary

Drawing on Richard Nisbett's research on the geographic origins of cognitive styles, this talk presents a practical playbook for applying holistic and analytic psychology to UX design and marketing strategy. It challenges the binary view of Eastern versus Western cognition by demonstrating that the overlap in human cognitive tendencies is far greater than any perceived divide, offering a more nuanced framework for design thinking.

## Key Takeaways

- Nisbett's framework contrasting ancient Greek logic with Confucian relational worldviews provides a useful but simplified lens — modern psychology emphasizes the substantial overlap in cognitive tendencies across cultures.
- Holistic cognition groups by overall atmosphere, context, and harmony, focusing on interconnectedness with the broader environment.
- Analytic cognition prioritizes breaking things into parts, grouping by distinct objects, attributes, and clear categories.
- Effective design strategy requires consciously switching between these lenses depending on the problem context rather than defaulting to one mode.
- Marketing and user experience benefit from understanding when to emphasize systemic harmony and when to highlight individual component features.
