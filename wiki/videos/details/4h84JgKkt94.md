## Summary

This Spanish-language video is a travel guide through Gyeongju, the millennial capital of the Silla dynasty (57 BC - 935 AD) in southeastern Korea, presented as "a museum without walls." It explores the UNESCO World Heritage royal tumuli burial mounds at Daereungwon Park, temples, palaces, and pagodas that are so integrated into daily life that walking through the city feels like strolling through galleries of history.

## Key Takeaways

- Gyeongju served as the capital of the Silla dynasty for nearly 1,000 years and is designated a UNESCO World Heritage site.
- The grass-covered hills at Daereungwon Park are not natural formations but royal burial mounds (tumuli) for Silla kings, queens, and nobility, numbering over 230.
- The city's temples, palaces, and pagodas are so integrated into modern daily life that the entire city functions as an open-air museum.
- The Silla dynasty achieved the unification of the Korean peninsula, with Gyeongju's golden age during the Unified Silla period.
