## Summary

This relocation dossier dismantles the idealized image of Costa Rica as an accessible paradise by exposing a 30–60% expatriate exodus rate driven by hidden cost-of-living overruns and infrastructure gaps. It walks through the legal foundations of the pensionado residency category, the DIEX process with Dirección General de Migración y Extranjería, and contrasts Costa Rica's territorial tax system — 0% income tax on foreign pensions, 13% VAT, and an annual property tax — against the U.S. worldwide model.

## Key Takeaways

- Between 30% and 60% of expatriates leave Costa Rica within their first five years, primarily due to hidden living costs and infrastructure deficiencies.
- The pensionado residency requires a guaranteed lifetime pension certified by the country of origin, plus a $250 DIEX processing fee.
- Supporting documents such as birth certificates and criminal records must be apostilled and no older than six months, with resolution taking three to six months.
- Pensionado residency must be renewed every two years and only requires a minimum physical presence of one day per year to remain valid.
- Costa Rica's territorial tax system exempts foreign-source pensions and investments from income tax while applying a 13% VAT and annual property tax locally.
