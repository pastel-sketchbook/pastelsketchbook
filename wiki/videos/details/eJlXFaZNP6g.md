## Summary

TigerStyle is presented as a disciplined software engineering philosophy that treats style as design, prioritizing safety, performance, and developer experience as three load-bearing pillars in that strict order. The session frames a zero technical debt mandate where rigorous upfront design — informed by NASA's Power of Ten rules and explicit control flow — is shown to dramatically reduce production fix costs compared to standard development trajectories.

## Key Takeaways

- Style is treated as design rather than aesthetics, fusing engineering rigor with artistic intuition into what the speaker calls biodigital jazz.
- An hour invested in design is positioned as worth months of production cost, motivating a zero technical debt mandate enforced at the design phase.
- Safety, performance, and developer experience are ranked in a strict descending hierarchy so that fundamental security is never sacrificed for speed or convenience.
- Memory latency spikes and exponential algorithms are categorically prohibited from shipping, reflecting a non-negotiable reliability bar.
- The Power of Ten rules anchor the safety pillar, with explicit control flow valued over cleverness to keep logic auditable and resistant to hidden errors.
