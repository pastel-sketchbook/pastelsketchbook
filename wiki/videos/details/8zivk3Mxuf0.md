# Navigating Growth and Taxes from 65 to 75

> [finance](../finance.md) · 1 views · Aug 13, 2026
> [Watch on YouTube](https://youtu.be/8zivk3Mxuf0)

## Summary

This talk is a retirement tax-planning playbook for the "golden decade" from ages 65 to 75, centered on using the income valley (ages 65–72) to diffuse required minimum distributions (RMDs) before they arrive. It walks through RMD start-age rules by birth year, the tax avalanche created when RMDs stack on top of pension and Social Security income, IRMAA Medicare surcharge traps, and Roth-conversion strategies that can permanently shrink mandatory distributions.

## Key Takeaways

- RMD start age depends on birth year — 73 for those born 1951–1959, and 75 for those born in 1960 or later (beginning 2033) — with the first RMD due by April 1 of the year after reaching RMD age and all subsequent RMDs due by December 31.
- The income valley (ages 65–72) is arguably the single most impactful tax-planning window: taxable income often falls to the $0–$40K range before Social Security and RMDs start, leaving large portions of the 12%/22% brackets available for Roth conversions, bracket filling, and capital-gain harvesting.
- RMDs stack on top of investment income, pensions, and up to 85%-taxable Social Security, often pushing retirees into the 22%, 24%, or 32% marginal brackets and over the IRMAA income thresholds ($13K single / $26K married filing jointly in MAGI), adding $800–$3,000 per year in Medicare costs.
- Taking the first RMD by December 31 of the year you turn 73 avoids the April 1 double-RMD trap, which would otherwise concentrate two distributions in one tax year, spike your bracket, and trigger IRMAA surcharges.
- A case study shows how converting $80K per year over seven years from a $1M IRA in the income valley cuts the RMD base to $440K, reduces annual RMDs from roughly $40K to $17.6K (a ~56% cut), and can save an estimated $100K–$300K in lifetime taxes.
