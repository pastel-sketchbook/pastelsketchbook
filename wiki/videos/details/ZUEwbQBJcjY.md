## Summary

This session maps the structural disconnect between where digital populations live, where cyber threats strike, and where defensive capital flows — China and India hold 2.3 billion users while the United States absorbs 25.5 percent of global attacks and outspends all tracked nations. It attributes the concentration to economic density, digital dependence, and geopolitical friction, models the breach-cost and regulatory spending feedback loop, examines South Korea's high-threat mid-tier archetype, and derives a multiplicative risk equation for national exposure.

## Key Takeaways

- Track where threats land rather than where users are: 2.3 billion Chinese and Indian internet users sit alongside single-digit-to-teens billions of security spend, while the third-ranked US market absorbs a quarter of all attacks.
- Attribute US concentration to a wealth amplifier triad — economic density, near-total digital dependence, and geopolitical centrality — sustaining roughly $95 billion in annual spend, six to seven times China's $13 billion.
- Model the self-reinforcing spending loop: average US breach costs above $9 to 10 million plus SEC disclosure and insurance mandates expand investment, which grows attack surfaces and restarts the cycle.
- Read South Korea as the high-threat mid-tier archetype: a $5.9 to 7.2 billion market compounding at 11 to 12 percent under persistent North Korean state pressure, while corporate InfoSec budgets hover near 0.1 percent of revenues.
- Calibrate national exposure as digital dependence multiplied by asset value multiplied by geopolitical friction — the formula that yields $95 billion for the US, about $13 billion behind China's sovereign firewall, and $7 billion for Korea.
