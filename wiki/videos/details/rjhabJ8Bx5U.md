## Summary

This deck documents the structural divergence of alternative asset management and the collapse of the traditional 60/40 portfolio model. It argues that persistent inflation, supply shocks, and a now-positive stock-bond correlation have uncoupled fixed income as an equity hedge, motivating a new 40/30/30 institutional allocation that captures the illiquidity premium across private equity, real assets, and private credit.

## Key Takeaways

- The classic inverse stock-bond correlation has effectively uncoupled in a higher-inflation regime, so fixed income no longer reliably hedges equity volatility for institutional portfolios.
- A 40/30/30 allocation (40% public equities, 30% bonds, 30% alternatives split equally between private equity, real assets, and private credit) lifts Sharpe ratio from 1.00 to 1.27 and adds ~1.3% nominal return per annum versus 60/40.
- Non-traditional supply shocks, persistent labor shortages, and energy-transition costs are framed as durable structural forces, not cyclical noise — permanently resetting the baseline risk-return landscape.
- Alternative asset management is not merely growing in volume but forging new operational paradigms (funding architecture, liquidity engineering) that increasingly separate it from traditional public-market frameworks.
- The illiquidity premium is positioned as a strategic inflation hedge, replacing duration as the primary diversifier in the modern institutional toolkit.
