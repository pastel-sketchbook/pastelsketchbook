## Summary

Grounded in the organizational-slack literature (Cyert & March, 1963; Bourgeois, 1981), this talk argues that AI shifts the unit of slack from headcount and budget to time and cognitive capacity, generating an estimated 1.3–1.8 full-time equivalents of freed time per employee. It shows how to convert that margin into durable advantage across four structural layers—cognitive and judgment, experimentation, orchestration and governance (including multi-agent systems with human–AI handoffs), and data and compute (API capacity, clean data reserves, security headroom)—while warning that unchecked AI adoption collapses into "work slop" of unverified outputs. Concrete mechanisms include AI agent sandbox environments, dedicated orchestrators, an AI Slack formula, and a three-horizon allocation funnel.

## Key Takeaways

- AI fundamentally shifts organizational slack from headcount and budget to time and cognitive capacity, generating an estimated 1.3–1.8 full-time equivalents per employee.
- The "lean paradox" shows that pushing toward zero slack guarantees systemic fragility, while the strategic cushion at the peak drives the most resilience, innovation, and performance.
- Induced demand from AI speed produces "work slop"—floods of low-quality reports, generic code, and flawed analytics that force constant human correction and erase genuine time savings.
- Protecting the cognitive buffer between the automation zone and strategic decision-making is essential, since bypassing it leads to unchecked system failure and negative return on investment.
- New metrology measuring percent automated, time margin generated, and reallocation rate replaces headcount and utilization metrics, steering AI savings into high-value strategy rather than more busy work.
