# Global Gigawatt: The Engineering & Architecture of Mega-Scale AI Data Centers

> [finance](../finance.md) · 34 views · Jul 29, 2026
> [Watch on YouTube](https://youtu.be/BZm2J9sLEp8)

## Summary

This talk surveys the engineering and architecture of mega-scale AI data centers, where multi-gigawatt "titans" (3,000–11,000 MW facilities like Fermi's Project Matador and SB Energy's Ports campus) and the 1,200–2,800 MW "hyperscale vanguard" are replacing traditional ~50 MW enterprise data centers. It maps the four dominant power strategies — private hypergrids, federal/brownfield redevelopment under FAST-41, behind-the-meter nuclear, and off-grid fuel cell microgrids — against hard physical constraints: 3–4 year turbine and transformer lead times, PJM interconnection queues pushing grid-reliant projects past 2029, and a forced shift to closed-loop liquid and air-hybrid cooling. Four US regional hubs (ERCOT Texas, the Rust Belt PJM corridor, the Mountain West, and Louisiana) each offer distinct power, latency, land, and water economics.

## Key Takeaways

- Scale leap: traditional enterprise data centers run at roughly 50 MW, while "titan" AI campuses demand 3,000 MW or more — Matador (Amarillo, ~11,000 MW), SB Energy Ports (Piketon, ~10,000 MW), and Meta Hyperion (Rayville, ~5,000 MW) — making traditional grid hookups impossible.
- Above ~1.5 GW developers must become their own utility: dedicated power plants, FAST-41 federal interventions, or massive private microgrid architectures; below it, a maximized heavy transmission substation caps out around 1,200–1,500 MW.
- Supply chain is the binding constraint for 2026–2027 launches: step-up transformers and heavy industrial gas turbines carry 3–4 year lead times, and PJM interconnection queues push grid-reliant projects to 2029 and beyond.
- Power strategy varies by player: Fermi/Meta run private hypergrids pairing on-site gas with massive solar and battery (sprint to base); AWS Salem draws zero-carbon baseline power directly from an existing nuclear station, bypassing the PJM queue entirely.
- Cooling transitions from evaporative to closed-loop liquid and dry/air-hybrid solutions for post-2028 campuses, because evaporative cooling strains local water supplies at mega-campus scale.
