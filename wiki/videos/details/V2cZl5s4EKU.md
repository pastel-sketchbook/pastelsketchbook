## Key Takeaways

- Korean and Spanish both have shallow orthography with five core vowels (a, e, i, o, u) that map nearly identically, giving Korean speakers a massive pronunciation advantage.
- Both Korean and Spanish are pro-drop languages that naturally omit subject pronouns, providing an intuitive structural similarity that accelerates acquisition.
- The "typological nexus" positions Spanish at the intersection of Romance, Germanic (English), and Korean linguistic frameworks, creating an accelerated acquisition zone for bilinguals.
- Monolingual English speakers face a harder path to Spanish pronunciation because English has deep orthography where a single letter can represent multiple sounds.
