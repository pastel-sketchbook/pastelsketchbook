## Summary

This video examines the phase-out of the American penny, covering the US Treasury's final production orders, the Common Sense Act introduced in April 2025 by Representatives Lisa McLain and Robert Garcia, and the economic concept of negative seigniorage where the manufacturing cost of a penny exceeds its one-cent face value. It explores the legislative framework for retail rounding and the broader implications for consumer transactions and government finances in an increasingly digital economy.

## Key Takeaways

- The US Treasury has placed its final penny orders, with the circulating supply expected to be fully exhausted by 2026.
- The Common Sense Act (April 2025, Reps. McLain and Garcia) formalizes cessation of penny production and establishes the legal framework for retail rounding.
- Every penny minted operates at a direct financial loss due to negative seigniorage — manufacturing cost exceeds the one-cent face value.
- The transition signals a broader shift toward digital payment infrastructure and away from low-denomination physical currency.
