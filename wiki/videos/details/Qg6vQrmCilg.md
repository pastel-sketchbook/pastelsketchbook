# Beyond the Ratio

## Summary

This video challenges the conventional reliance on WCAG 2.x contrast ratios and introduces the Accessible Perceptual Contrast Algorithm (APCA) as a more human-centered approach to accessibility. It argues that maximum contrast (pure black on white) actually harms readability on self-illuminating screens due to veiling glare, and explores how WCAG 3.0 incorporates typography-aware, perceptually accurate contrast measurement for dark mode and inclusive design.

## Key Takeaways

- Pure 21:1 contrast ratio causes visual vibration and veiling glare on screens; soft gray text on off-white backgrounds reduces fatigue while maintaining legibility.
- WCAG 2.x's contrast formula ignores typography entirely, meaning a passing ratio can still be unreadable at small font sizes or thin weights.
- APCA replaces the simple luminance ratio with a perceptually weighted algorithm that accounts for font size, weight, and polarity (light-on-dark vs dark-on-light).
- Dark mode requires its own contrast considerations because human vision perceives light text on dark backgrounds differently than the inverse.
- WCAG 3.0 is reshaping accessibility standards by moving from rigid mathematical thresholds toward nuanced, context-aware contrast guidelines.
