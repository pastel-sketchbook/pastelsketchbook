# The Exchange Rate of Your Life

## Summary

This presentation provides a framework for understanding the relationship between time and money through the concept of "temporal arbitrage." It introduces three stages of wealth evolution—survival (trading time for money), leverage (systems that earn while you sleep), and freedom (time as the ultimate currency)—and argues that optimizing your real hourly rate and thinking in terms of a 10-year dividend are essential for financial and personal fulfillment. The central thesis, drawn from Thoreau, is that the true cost of anything is the amount of life exchanged for it.

## Key Takeaways

- Money is renewable and can be recovered after total loss, but time is non-renewable—every second spent earning is a second of existence permanently consumed.
- The three stages of wealth evolution are survival (trading time for money), leverage (building systems that generate income independently), and freedom (where time becomes the primary currency).
- The "10-year dividend" framework encourages evaluating decisions based on their compounded returns over a decade rather than their immediate payoff.
- Calculating your real hourly rate (including commute, preparation, and recovery time) reveals whether your current exchange rate of life-for-money is actually optimized.
