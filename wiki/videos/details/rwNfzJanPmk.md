## Summary

This Spanish-language video is a travel diary exploring Ganghwa Island, South Korea's fifth-largest island, described as an "open-air museum" located just one hour from Seoul. It covers the island's strategic historical significance at the confluence of the Han River and the sea, its role as a provisional capital during the Goryeo and Joseon dynasties, and the 11th-century Ganghwa Fortress. The island now embraces "healing tourism" combining ancient military heritage with coastal serenity.

## Key Takeaways

- Ganghwa Island served as a provisional capital during the Goryeo and Joseon dynasties and housed royal archives due to its perceived impregnability.
- The Ganghwa Fortress dates to the 11th century and the island was historically known as "the land of safety" for sheltering royalty during wartime.
- Located at coordinates 37.75°N, 126.48°E where the Han River meets the sea, Ganghwa is only one hour from modern Seoul yet feels centuries removed.
- The island has evolved toward "healing tourism" (turismo de curación), blending ancient military heritage with coastal relaxation.
