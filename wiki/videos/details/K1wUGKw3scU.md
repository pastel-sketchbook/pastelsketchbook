## Summary

South Korea's KRW 1,100 trillion (~$880B) national initiative reframes AI hardware leadership as an execution problem rather than a capital one, building a tightly synchronized ecosystem of mega-fab clusters, AI data centers, and physical AI. The blueprint couples advanced memory (HBM/DRAM) capacity with power, water, and land infrastructure plus a layered incentive stack, treating preemptive manufacturing capacity as the ultimate competitive moat.

## Key Takeaways

- The strategy rests on a "triple-axis" closed-loop engine — semiconductors as raw material, AI data centers as token factories, and physical AI as real-world application — that continuously reinforces itself.
- HBM's vertical stacking and through-silicon-via design yield fewer usable dies per wafer, making a larger physical fab footprint the decisive constraint for maintaining market share in the AI era.
- A KRW 1,100 trillion mobilization across three regional hubs (Yongin/Pyeongtaek, Cheongju, and the Honam southwestern cluster) accelerates fab timelines by up to 12 years while rebalancing development away from the Seoul metropolitan area.
- A four-layer capital stack — R&D tax credits, equipment investment credits, up to 75% cash grants, and customs duty exemptions — is engineered so execution speed, not capital availability, becomes the binding constraint.
- The industry shift from cyclical memory production toward system semiconductors (GPUs, NPUs, ASICs, chiplets, 3D stacking) is exemplified by AI chip startups like Rebellions and ZenoCube.
