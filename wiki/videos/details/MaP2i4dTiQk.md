## Summary

Based on Kent Beck's experiments, this video explores how LLM-powered augmented coding is reshaping software design, arguing that we are in the "horseless carriage" stage where developers use AI to replicate existing coding patterns rather than discovering its native form. It draws historical parallels to how the telegraph became radio and the carriage became the automobile, suggesting that LLM code generation will similarly evolve beyond current paradigms. The presentation examines how to harness AI as an augmented coding partner while the industry searches for its transformative end state.

## Key Takeaways

- We are in the "horseless carriage" phase of LLM-assisted coding, using AI to replicate existing development workflows rather than discovering fundamentally new approaches to software design.
- Historical precedent shows that transformative technologies (telegraph to radio, carriage to automobile, physical mail to email) always pass through a phase of being understood through old mental frames before their native form emerges.
- Kent Beck's experiments suggest that augmented coding—where the developer and AI collaborate—produces better results than either fully manual or fully automated code generation.
- The key question is not whether AI will transform software development, but what the "native form" of LLM-assisted software design will ultimately look like.
