## Key Takeaways

- Judge returns by risk efficiency, not absolute gain: unlevered equities (QQQ, SPY) outperformed levered products (TQQQ, UPRO) on a Sharpe basis because daily resets and compounding drag — volatility decay — turn 50%-plus volatility into structural cost; exclude leverage (TQQQ, SOXL, SSO) from long-term holds entirely.
- Engineer the core around three models: Balanced Growth (60% SPY + 25% GLD + 15% QQQ/SOXX targets 8-10% CAGR with gold buffering equity shocks); the Golden Butterfly (five equal 20% sleeves — VTI, VBR, TLT, SHY, GLD) lands ~8.7-8.8% CAGR with 17-19.5% max drawdown and a 0.7-0.9 Sharpe; the Permanent Portfolio (four equal 25% quadrants — stocks, long bonds, gold, short-term liquidity) targets 7-9% CAGR while capping drawdowns at 15-18%.
- Map assets to the four economic seasons instead of predicting them: equities for prosperity, long-term Treasuries (TLT) for deflation, gold for inflation, and short bonds/cash (SHY, BIL) for recessions — diversified structures survive dot-com, 2008, and 2022-class shocks while 100% equity demands tolerating 50%+ drawdowns even at ~12% CAGR.
- Match your architecture to drawdown tolerance: as equity exposure steps down from 100% to 25%, CAGR compresses from ~12% to 7-10% but max drawdowns compress to under 20% — prioritizing capital preservation and sustainable withdrawal rates over absolute growth.
- Execute on four practical pillars: keep implementation simple with liquid, low-cost ETFs (blended Golden Butterfly ≈ 0.16% expense ratio); rebalance annually each January — or when any sleeve drifts ±5% — directing new contributions to underweights to avoid taxable sales; hold taxable assets like GLD inside IRAs/401(k)s; and treat leveraged ETFs as tactical trading tools, never load-bearing pillars.
