# The Mystery of the Nickel Coke

> [finance](../finance.md) · 8 views · Apr 10, 2026
> [Watch on YouTube](https://youtu.be/l9UUqD2Bz2E)

## Summary

This video examines the economic anomaly of Coca-Cola maintaining a fixed 5-cent price for over 70 years (1886-1959) while nearly all other consumer goods experienced significant inflation. Using 140 years of price data on soda, milk, and daily essentials, it explores the mechanisms behind this extreme price rigidity. The analysis covers how vending machine technology, long-term syrup contracts, and strategic pricing decisions created an exception to standard inflationary economics.

## Key Takeaways

- Between 1913 and 2013, basic staples like bread increased over 25x in price (6 cents to $1.42), while Coca-Cola held at 5 cents for most of that period.
- The nickel price persisted partly because vending machines of the era only accepted single coins, creating a mechanical barrier to price adjustment.
- A nickel in 1886 had vastly more purchasing power than in 1959, meaning Coca-Cola's real revenue per bottle declined dramatically over the 73-year fixed-price period.
- The case study illustrates how contractual obligations, distribution technology, and consumer psychology can override standard supply-demand pricing models.
