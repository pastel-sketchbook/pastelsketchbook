## Summary

This talk dissects the physical infrastructure economy behind modern AI, reframing AI as a heavy industry built on megawatts (power), molecules (cooling and materials), and megabits (optical fiber). It explains the site-selection constraints for AI campuses — gigawatt-scale power, dual long-haul fiber corridors, and metro fiber density — catalogs the eight physical choke points that stall data movement, and maps the emerging geography of compute from tier-1 AI belts like the Texas Triangle to legacy no-build zones.

## Key Takeaways

- AI is increasingly a civil-engineering problem: success depends on rights-of-way, gigawatt power, and fiber routes, not just algorithmic innovation.
- A viable AI campus must sit at the triple intersection of two physically separated long-haul fiber corridors (20-50 miles apart), 8-12 diverse metro fiber entry points, and 500MW-5GW of power capacity.
- Eight physical choke points — cross-connect port exhaustion, 400GZR/800G coherent optics shortages, exhausted metro duct banks, trenching limits, fiber-splicer labor gaps, rights-of-way competition, long-haul dark-fiber scarcity, and parallel routing mandates — stall scaling even when capital is abundant.
- The geography of compute is shifting: tier-1 AI belts (Texas Triangle, Phoenix, Chicago-Columbus) are winning infrastructure buildouts while the Bay Area, Northeast Corridor, and Pacific Northwest urban cores become no-build zones.
- The AI refinery model — shale gas molecules converted to microgrid megawatts, powering hyperscale compute, exported as fiber megabits — is repositioning regions like Texas to export compute the way they once exported crude.
