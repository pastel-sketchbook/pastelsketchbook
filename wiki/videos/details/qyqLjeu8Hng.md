## Summary

This Korean-language video examines whether the "3,000-hour rule" for English listening mastery is scientifically valid, concluding that it works but only with systematic, deliberate practice — not passive exposure. It introduces the concept of a "sound map" (neural pathways that map English sounds to meaning) and explains why Korean learners who studied English visually through grammar books lack this auditory foundation. The video contrasts ineffective passive listening (background radio, incomprehensible content) with effective active methods (focused listening at 70-80% comprehension level with transcript verification).

## Key Takeaways

- The 3,000-hour rule for English listening works, but only with systematic deliberate practice, not passive background exposure.
- A "sound map" is the neural circuit that instantly maps heard English sounds to words and meanings; most Korean learners lack this because they studied English visually.
- Effective listening requires content at 70-80% comprehension level with focused attention and transcript-based verification of sound-to-text connections.
- Passive methods like background English radio or watching incomprehensible content without subtitles waste time and build no neural pathways.
