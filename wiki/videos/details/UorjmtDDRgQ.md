## Summary

This talk traces the cultural and historical diffusion of folded-dough foods — from the Chinese protodumpling along the Silk Road to Central Asian manti, Himalayan momos, Eastern European pierogi, Italian tortellini, and Latin American empanadas. It frames Turkic and Mongol nomads as culinary pollinators whose movements seeded regional adaptations, and follows the linguistic drift of the proto-root *manty* across Turkish, Armenian, and Himalayan languages.

## Key Takeaways

- Folded dough functions as a near-universal comfort food whose core architecture recurs independently across East Asia, Europe, and the Americas.
- The Chinese protodumpling is identified as the foundational form that traveled outward along the Silk Road to become regional specialties such as manti and mantou.
- Turkic and Mongol nomadic groups acted as active agents of culinary cross-pollination, carrying recipes westward into Europe, eastward to Korea, and across Central Asia.
- Mongolian buuz serves as an evolutionary branching point, splitting into westward Central Asian adaptations and eastward Korean integrations.
- Linguistic anchors like the proto-root *manty* show systematic fracture and adaptation as the tradition migrated across the Eurasian continent.
