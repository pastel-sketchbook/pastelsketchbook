## Summary

This video presents a GDPR engineering blueprint, translating privacy-by-design principles into concrete architectural patterns. It covers the six core data privacy principles—accountability, data minimization, purpose limitation, lawful basis, transparency, and security—and maps each to implementation strategies including consent management, DPIA methodology, cross-border data transfer mechanisms, breach notification workflows, anonymization gateways, and privacy-enhancing technologies (PETs).

## Key Takeaways

- Privacy-by-design is an architectural mandate, not a compliance checkbox—it must be embedded into system design from the data model layer upward.
- The six GDPR principles (accountability, data minimization, purpose limitation, lawful basis, transparency, security) each demand specific engineering controls, not just policy documents.
- DPIAs provide a structured methodology for identifying and mitigating privacy risks before systems go live, serving as both a compliance artifact and an architectural review tool.
- Anonymization gateways and PETs (differential privacy, homomorphic encryption, secure enclaves) enable data utility while preserving individual privacy rights across jurisdictional boundaries.
