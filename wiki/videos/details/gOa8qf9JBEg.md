## Summary

This session engineers forced human-in-the-loop feedback systems for credit and risk decisioning in high-stakes financial environments. It models AI as a structural amplifier across speed, justification, and path dependency, mandates structural intervention gates over optional review, layers input redesign with forced feedback and rollback architecture, and operationalizes governance through risk-tiered gates, counterfactual review, dual logging with post-decision audits, smart sampling, time-boxed escalation, expected-loss framing, dynamic gray zones, override-rate targets, and a monthly operating cycle.

## Key Takeaways

- Treat AI as an amplifier that compounds flawed inputs across execution speed, data-driven justification authority, and training-set path dependency rather than a neutral corrective mechanism.
- Enforce oversight structurally with default-locked pipelines requiring explicit human intervention instead of optional reviews that collapse under fatigue and automation bias.
- Calibrate risk tiers operationally with auto-approval plus 5 to 10 percent sampling for low risk, mandatory single signoff for medium risk, four-eyes separation for high risk, and full manual underwriting for extreme anomalies.
- Govern with expected loss by multiplying probability of default by exposure at default by loss given default so dollar-denominated risk rather than abstract scores dictates automation boundaries.
- Size dynamic gray zones at 30 to 35 percent of application volume with immediate 5 percent expansion on risk signals, cautious quarterly contraction, a 15 percent floor, and override rates held in the 15 to 35 percent healthy band.
