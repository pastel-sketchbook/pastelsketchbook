## Summary

This video examines the NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0, highlighting its shift from prescriptive checklists to a taxonomy of outcomes that manages cybersecurity as a unified enterprise risk discipline. It covers the framework's three foundational components (Core, Profiles, and Tiers) and explains how organizations of any size can use it to assess posture gaps, prioritize actions, and establish a common security language.

## Key Takeaways

- CSF 2.0 provides a taxonomy of high-level cybersecurity outcomes rather than a prescriptive checklist, applicable to any organization regardless of size or sector.
- The framework is built on three components: the Core (functions, categories, subcategories), Profiles (current vs. target posture), and Tiers (maturity levels).
- Organizations use CSF 2.0 to understand and assess gaps, prioritize work aligned with mission and risk expectations, and communicate using a common language across teams.
- The framework marks a pivot from isolated security measures toward managing cybersecurity as an integrated enterprise risk discipline.
