## Summary

This presentation explores Bayesian inference as a mathematically optimal framework for decision-making and reasoning under uncertainty. It contrasts the iterative 'degree of belief' approach of Bayesian statistics with the 'fixed frequency' model of frequentist statistics, demonstrating how new evidence dynamically updates our understanding of reality.

## Key Takeaways

- Bayesian inference operates as a continuous iterative cycle where the posterior belief of one calculation becomes the prior for the next update.
- The medical testing paradox illustrates the base rate fallacy, showing that the rarity of a condition often exerts more influence on the final probability than the accuracy of the test itself.
- Naive Bayes classifiers enable efficient classification by treating features as independent variables, allowing for rapid posterior probability calculations in applications like spam filtering.
- The fundamental architectural divide in statistics is that frequentists treat parameters as fixed constants, while Bayesians treat parameters as random variables with probability distributions.
- Practical engineering implementations of Bayesian logic include Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) in robotics, BM25 ranking in search engines, and early-stopping in A/B testing.
