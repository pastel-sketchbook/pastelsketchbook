## Summary

This video provides a comprehensive walkthrough of the modern 3D computer graphics pipeline, covering the four algorithmic pillars: geometry generation via parametric curves, rasterization using Bresenham's line algorithm, ray tracing through quadratic intersection equations, and Lambertian diffuse lighting. It explains the mathematical and physical foundations that transform digital information into rendered visual experiences.

## Key Takeaways

- The graphics pipeline rests on four pillars: geometry generation (parametric curves), rasterization (Bresenham's algorithm), ray tracing (quadratic intersection), and Lambertian diffuse lighting.
- Rasterization converts 2D primitives into discrete pixels using fast integer math, avoiding expensive floating-point operations.
- Ray tracing determines surface intersections by solving quadratic equations where a positive discriminant confirms a hit.
- Lambertian reflection is computed via the dot product of the surface normal and light direction to simulate realistic lighting.
