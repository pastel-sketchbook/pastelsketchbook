In  terminal user  interface  development,  we  are moving  from  an  imperative  model  to  a declarative  one.  Traditionally,  building TUIs  required  an  imperative  approach, which  often  felt  like  fighting  for control.  You  were  responsible  for manually  orchestrating  individual commands,  entering  the  alternate  screen, hiding  the  cursor,  or  enabling  mouse support.  As  applications  grew,  these manual  calls  became  a  tangled  web  of instructions  that  were  difficult  to manage  and  debug.  Bubble  T  shifts  this paradigm  toward  a  unified  declarative view.  Instead  of  managing  the  how  of terminal  manipulation,  you  focus  on  the what,  you  define  your  application  state and  that  state  flows  through  a streamlined  process  to  produce  the  final view.  You  simply  declare  what  you  want the  interface  to  look  like  at  any  given moment  and  the  framework  handles  the complex  terminal  orchestration  on  your behalf.
