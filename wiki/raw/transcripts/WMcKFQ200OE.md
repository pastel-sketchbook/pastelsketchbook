Welcome  everyone.  It  is  a  pleasure  to have  you  here  for  our  presentation  on the  Flutter  and  Dart  2026  roadmap.  This session  is  based  on  the  official  update released  in  February  2026  by  Emma Turski,  focusing  on  our  vision  for building  the  future  of  agentic highfidelity  applications.  As  the digital  landscape  evolves,  our  focus remains  on  providing  developers  with  the tools  to  create  seamless,  intelligent, and  visually  stunning  experiences  across all  platforms. Today  we  will  explore  the  strategic pillars  and  architectural  advancements that  will  define  the  next  era  of development  within  the  Flutter  and  Dart ecosystem.  Our  mission  is  centered  on  a singular  goal  to  create  the  most popular,  fastest  growing  and  highest productivity  multiplatform  UI  framework. We  aim  to  set  the  standard  for  developer efficiency  while  ensuring  our  tools remain  versatile  across  every  major platform.  As  we  look  ahead,  it  is important  to  provide  a  note  on transparency  regarding  our  roadmap.  The plans  we  present  represent  an aspirational  strategy  rather  than  a rigid  schedule.  In  the  dynamic  world  of open  source,  priorities  may  adapt throughout  the  year  as  we  respond  to  new challenges  and  community  feedback.  We ask  that  you  view  this  road  map  as  a sincere  statement  of  our  intent  and vision,  acknowledging  that  it  is  subject to  change  as  we  strive  for  excellence. The  executive  summary  highlights  the four  strategic  pillars  of  our  evolution. First,  we  focus  on  highfidelity multiplatform  performance  utilizing impeller  and  was  to  achieve  native  speed and  responsiveness  across  all  devices. Second,  we  are  advancing  Gen  UI  and agentic  apps  where  AI  interfaces  and  the A2  UI  protocol  enable  the  creation  of ephemeral  code  for  dynamic  user experiences.  Third,  we  are  expanding into  full  stack  Dart,  unifying  the development  process  by  bringing  the language  to  cloud  functions,  backends, and  genkit.  Finally,  we  are  committed  to a  sustainable  ecosystem  through  improved governance,  strategic  decoupling,  and  a steadfast  dedication  to  open-source, ensuring  the  long-term  health  and scalability  of  our  community.  Our  first pillar  focuses  on  achieving  highfidelity rendering  on  mobile  through  a fundamental  architectural  shift.  We  have moved  from  our  legacy  Skia  engine  to  the modern  impeller  backend.  As  illustrated by  the  waveforms,  the  legacy  system often  produced  inconsistent  frame delivery  resulting  in  the  jank represented  by  the  jagged  line  on  the left.  In  contrast,  Impeller  provides  the fluid,  consistent  performance  shown  by the  smooth  sine  wave  on  the  right.  The migration  is  now  complete  and  Impeller is  officially  the  default  rendering engine  on  Android.  We  are  also  advancing our  legacy  removal  process  by deprecating  the  Skia  backend  on  Android 10  and  above.  For  end  users,  this transition  results  in  perfectly  smooth animations,  the  elimination  of  Jenk,  and significantly  faster  application  startup times.  This  modernization  ensures  we  are platform  ready  with  day  zero  support  for the  latest  operating  systems,  including iOS  and  Android  17.  Pillar  1  focuses  on delivering  native  performance  across  web and  desktop  platforms.  First,  we  are making  web  assembly  the  default  for  web deployment.  This  transition  allows  WASOM to  serve  as  the  standard  for  delivering native  quality  performance  and highfidelity  experiences  directly  within the  browser.  On  the  desktop  front,  we are  deepening  our  integration  through  a continued  partnership  with  Canonicle. This  collaboration  specifically  targets the  advancement  of  multi-wind  support  on Ubuntu,  ensuring  that  desktop applications  feel  integrated  and performant  across  complex  workflows. Pillar  2  focuses  on  the  Genui revolution,  marking  a  fundamental  shift from  traditional  pre-built  screens toward  intelligent  adaptive  interfaces. Rather  than  relying  on  static  layouts, this  approach  begins  with  the  user's intent.  This  intent  is  processed  by  the model,  an  AIdriven  engine  that dynamically  generates  a  customized interface.  As  illustrated,  individual components  are  assembled  in  real  time  to create  an  adaptive  UI  layout  tailored  to the  specific  context  of  the  interaction. To  bring  this  vision  to  life,  we  are leveraging  two  core  technologies.  The Flutter  Gen  UI  SDK,  a  comprehensive toolkit  for  building  agentive  user interfaces,  and  the  A2  UI  protocol, which  facilitates  the  dynamic  generation of  these  personalized  experiences. Pillar  2  focuses  on  ephemeral  code delivery  which  addresses  a  fundamental challenge  for  agentic  applications.  The need  for  ondemand  logic.  Traditional application  models  often  struggle  with the  latency  and  friction  of  manual updates.  By  utilizing  interpreted  bite code,  we  can  stream  logic  directly  from the  cloud  to  a  specialized  app  runtime on  the  user's  device.  This  process allows  specific  portions  of  an application  to  load  instantly  when needed,  completely  bypassing  the requirement  for  traditional  app  store updates  or  installations. This  architecture  ensures  that  agentic apps  remain  lightweight  and  highly adaptable,  delivering  the  exact functionality  required  in  real  time. Pillar  3  focuses  on  full  stack  Dart, representing  a  major  advancement  in allowing  developers  to  utilize  a  single language  across  their  entire  development stack.  With  the  introduction  of  Dart Cloud  Functions  for  Firebase, applications  can  achieve  high performance  through  exceptionally  fast cold  starts  of  approximately  10 milliseconds.  Additionally,  the  Google Cloud  SDK  now  enables  developers  to connect  and  build  backends  on  Google Cloud  with  greater  ease.  Finally, through  Genkit  support,  you  can  build sophisticated  AI  features  directly  in Dart.  This  integration  empowers  teams  to build  more  efficiently  by  unifying  their codebase  from  the  client  side  to  the cloud.  Moving  on  to  pillar  four,  we  are focusing  on  an  AI  re-imagined  developer experience.  This  phase  of  our  strategy is  dedicated  to  deeply  integrating artificial  intelligence  into  the  actual tools  and  workflows  developers  use everyday.  First,  with  a  combination  of Gemini  CLI  and  anti-gravity,  we  have enabled  stateful  hot  reload  to  work seamlessly  with  AI  coding  agents.  This means  that  as  an  AI  agent  proposes  and applies  code  changes,  the  application maintains  its  state,  allowing  for  a  much faster  and  more  intuitive  iterative process.  Second,  we  are  introducing  the model  context  protocol  or  MCP.  This creates  a  direct  line  of  communication between  AI  agents  and  the  Dart  analyzer. By  leveraging  the  analyzer's  deep understanding  of  the  codebase,  AI  agents can  now  perform  complex  refactors securely  and  accurately,  ensuring  that the  generated  code  is  not  only functional,  but  also  adheres  to established  patterns  and  type  safety. Modern  syntax  and  compiled  performance are  central  to  the  latest  updates.  We have  introduced  primary  constructors  to provide  more  streamlined  class declarations,  significantly  reducing boilerplate  code.  Additionally,  the  new augmentations  feature  simplifies  the code  generation  process,  making  it  more intuitive  for  developers  for  those managing  large-scale  applications.  The refactoring  of  the  analyzer  delivers enhanced  performance  and  a  more responsive  development  experience. Finally,  we  have  implemented  critical tooling  improvements  specifically targeting  buildrunner  and  dart  wom compilation  to  optimize  both  build  times and  execution  efficiency.  Sustainable open-source  and  governance  are fundamental  to  the  long-term  success  of the  Flutter  ecosystem.  To  achieve  this, we  are  implementing  several  strategic shifts.  First,  we  are  decoupling  key components  such  as  material  and cubertino  into  standalone  packages.  This modular  approach  allows  for  faster iterations  and  a  leaner  core  framework. Second,  we  are  enhancing  extensibility by  enabling  the  engine  to  support outoftree  platform  authoring,  making  it easier  for  third  parties  to  build  and maintain  platform  specific  integrations. Finally,  we  are  expanding  our  advisory boards,  specifically  the  GDE,  CAB,  and PAB  networks  to  ensure  diverse perspectives  guide  our  roadmap.  As illustrated  in  the  diagram,  Flutter thrives  through  a  circular  flow  of contributions  and  collaboration  between Google,  our  strategic  partners,  and  the broader  community,  ensuring  a  robust  and self-sustaining  future  for  the  platform.
