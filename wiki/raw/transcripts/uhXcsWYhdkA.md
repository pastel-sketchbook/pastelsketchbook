Welcome.  Today  we  are  pleased  to  present the  strategic  roadmap  for  data classification,  a  comprehensive implementation  guide  based  on  NIST special  publication  1839. This  work  stems  from  the  national  cyber security  center  of  excellence  data classification  practices  project  aimed at  helping  organizations  better understand  and  manage  their  data  assets. As  shown  in  the  illustration,  our  goal is  to  bring  order  to  complex  data streams  by  housing  them  within  a  secure structured  framework. The graphic  before  you  illustrates  a critical  challenge  facing  modern organizations,  the  massive  volume  of unstructured  data.  While  structured  data found  in  databases  typically  represents only  10  to  20%  of  an  enterprises  digital footprint,  the  remaining  80  to  90% consists  of  unstructured  content  such  as documents,  emails,  and  PDFs.  This  hidden mass  is  often  referred  to  as  dark  data because  it  remains  unknown,  unmanaged, and  invisible  to  traditional  oversight. As  noted  by  NIST,  the  primary organizational  risk  lies  beneath  the surface.  Without  proper  classification, this  data  cannot  be  effectively protected,  migrated,  or  utilized,  posing a  significant  hurdle  for  both  security and  operational  efficiency. Classification  is  the  fundamental prerequisite  for  modern  security  and  the essential  foundation  for  adopting emerging  technologies.  To  understand  its importance,  we  must  look  at  how  it enables  three  critical  pillars  of current  and  future  infrastructure. First,  within  a  zero  trust  architecture, the  concept  of  never  trust,  always verify  relies  entirely  on  knowing  what we  are  protecting.  Data  must  be accurately  labeled  so  that  access  can  be dynamically  authorized  based  on  its specific  sensitivity  level.  Without  this classification,  a  zero  trust  model cannot  make  informed  granular  decisions about  who  should  see  what.  Next,  as  we integrate  artificial  intelligence  into our  workflows,  data  classification becomes  a  safety  requirement.  AI  model training  requires  clean  labeled  data sets  to  function  reliably.  By classifying  our  data,  we  ensure  that models  are  trained  on  appropriate information  which  helps  maintain  safety and  prevents  the  risk  of  model poisoning.  Finally,  we  must  look  toward the  future  with  quantum  safe cryptography.  As  quantum  computing advances,  we  face  the  massive  task  of updating  our  encryption  standards. Classification  allows  organizations  to take  a  risk-based  approach,  prioritizing these  vital  encryption  updates  for  their most  sensitive  assets  first.  Ultimately, data  classification  is  the  foundational dependency  that  allows  us  to  navigate and  secure  the  evolving  technological landscape.  To  validate  our  proposed practices,  the  NCCE  collaborated  with industry  leaders  to  build  the  functional lab  environment  shown  here.  Our  primary goal  is  to  demonstrate  the  complete  life cycle  of  discovering,  identifying,  and labeling  data  across  network  file  shares and  endpoints.  The  architecture  centers on  a  synthetic  data  host  which  serves  as the  data  repository  for  the  various testing  environments.
